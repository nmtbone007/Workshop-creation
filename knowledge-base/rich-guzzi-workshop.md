# Rich Guzzi Workshop Notes (2025)

Ideas from a session of Rich Guzzi's live workshop last year. The session covers selling in person: carry every offer on your phone, close through text, and deliver digitally. The raw transcript is in [transcripts/rich-guzzi-2025.md](transcripts/rich-guzzi-2025.md). The recording quality was poor, so some numbers below are best readings of garbled speech.

Tag: **[Primary]** for what Guzzi taught, **[Self-reported]** for his results.

---

## The visibility stack

Guzzi draws three layers: the website on top, the CRM in the middle, and you at the bottom. When someone looks you up, they should find the website right away, and the CRM handles everything after that. He treats the CRM as the control center. [Primary]

He also runs a mock radio show and on-camera practice during the workshop. Students dress for camera on day two and record again on the last day so they can compare. [Primary]

## Be a walking lead machine

- People buy your promise to solve their problem more than they buy the product. [Primary]
- Successful buyers decide fast and don't change their minds. Spot them and give them a way to act on the spot. [Primary]
- Sell everywhere: the supermarket, a bookstore's self-help aisle, after a show. He calls this "hand-to-hand combat." [Primary]

## One checkout link per offer, saved on your phone

1. Build a checkout form in the CRM for every product. Some products get two: one for pay-in-full and one for a payment plan. His example: $1,000 in full, or $250 now with $250 billed automatically each month for three months. [Primary]
2. Copy each form's unique link into a single master note on your phone. Guzzi says he has about 100. [Self-reported]
3. When a conversation turns to an offer, open the note, tap the link, and text it to the person with a line about what you discussed. ("Hey Wayne, here's the hypnosis class we were talking about. Let me know if you have questions.") [Primary]
4. Your text thread becomes your to-do list. When the CRM texts you that someone paid, swipe them off the list. Follow up with everyone else. [Primary]

When you meet someone, ask for their number and type it into a new text yourself. Write the message as a note to them that also reminds you of the conversation. ("Great meeting you. Let's talk next week, I have the perfect plan for you.") [Primary]

## Let the system bill payment plans

Automatic billing keeps every buyer on the same schedule. Manual billing slips by a week, buyers lose momentum, and some stop paying. Guzzi says each person has a dollar number in their head; once the balance owed passes it, they quit paying. Keep credit short and don't push buyers into financing terms you wouldn't sign yourself. [Primary]

## Ladder your events

Sell the next date while the current one fills. He had 15 tickets sold for an August 5 to 10 event and a second funnel already running for the next date, so a sold-out event rolls straight into the next. [Primary, Self-reported]

## The digital delivery app

Guzzi paid a developer (he estimates about $50,000) to build a phone app holding his whole audio library. He picks a program, taps checkout, enters the buyer's phone number, and the program lands on their phone. He takes the money on the spot. No inventory, shipping, or printing. [Self-reported]

He built it after a cruise-ship show where buyers left a 400-person product line and later asked him for programs on the pool deck. [Self-reported]

The same app can send an electronic press kit. After a show, a real estate broker asked him to speak to 50 agents. Guzzi texted a confirmation plus the press kit for that talk. The broker watched it in the parking lot and expected Guzzi's call the next day. Guzzi calls this "spin-off business": deals that come from work you're already doing. [Self-reported]

## Product ladder for an audio library

Record 15 programs to start. [Primary]

| Tier | Example | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Free lead magnet | One program to give away | $0 |
| Low-cost entry | A sleep program | about $5 |
| Single programs | Stop smoking, weight loss | $20 each |
| Bundle | 12 programs | $100 ("the sweet spot") |

Sell the high-demand program, like stop smoking, on its own as well. Buyers who want it often won't look for it inside a bundle. [Primary]

Build two stacks. The general stack covers the problems that bring in about 95% of clients, and Guzzi suggests copying his. The niche stack has 12 programs for your specialty, such as peak performance for executives or speakers. [Primary]

## Recording the programs

- **Simple way:** a handheld digital recorder from a music store, recorded inside a clothes closet to deaden sound. [Primary]
- **Pro way:** a good microphone into recording software on a laptop, still in a quiet room or closet. [Primary]
- Record the induction once, then a daytime outro and a nighttime outro, then all the middle sections. An engineer splices each middle between an induction and an outro, adds music, fades, and removes clicks. [Primary]
- His music starts at 80 beats per minute, the average resting heart rate, and slows to about 60 so the listener's heart rate follows. He also offers versions at solfeggio frequencies such as 417 Hz. [Primary]
- He uses AI to draft scripts and says he has about 1,000. [Self-reported]

## Scoring: seven is perfect

Guzzi scores performances out of ten, with seven as a perfect score. The top three points are reserved for nights when everything lines up. [Primary]

He sorts shows into four levels by how the client reacts afterward. A poor show ends with a haggle over pay. After a good show the client is friendly. A great show gets a "let's do this again." An excellent show has the client booking the next date before you leave. Clients book on the spot when they see excellent work. [Primary]

A pro keeps working until they get it right. A master keeps working until they can't get it wrong. [Primary]

## Ideas to use in this workshop

- Create a checkout link for the workshop, the order bump, and the main offer, including a payment-plan version. Save all of them in one note on your phone. (Step 3)
- Text the checkout link during every in-person conversation, then use the text thread as the follow-up list. (Step 4, Step 5)
- Put payment plans on automatic billing in the CRM. (Step 1: make it safe)
- Open sales for the next workshop date before the current one ends. (Step 5)
- Offer a free or low-cost digital product as a lead magnet. (Step 4)

# Playbook by Checklist Step

The expert ideas, sorted into the five Workshop Launch Formula steps. Each line names its source so you can trace it in [experts.md](experts.md).

---

## Step 1: Dial in the offer

- **Score the offer with the Value Equation.** Rate your dream outcome and the buyer's belief they'll get it. Then find ways to cut the time to the first result and the effort required. (Hormozi)
- **List every obstacle, then answer each one.** Each answer becomes a deliverable. Cut whatever costs you a lot and adds little. (Hormozi)
- **Stack the offer.** Write each component with a value next to it so the price looks small against the total. (Brunson, Hormozi)
- **Make it safe.** Choose one: a money-back guarantee, a better-than-money-back guarantee where you keep working with them, a paid trial, or a payment plan. (Hormozi, Burch)
- **Test demand before building.** Sell founding spots to the workshop audience, then build the program with them. (McLaren, Walker's seed launch)
- **Add a high-ticket tier.** A small, capped, premium seat above the main offer lifts average revenue per buyer. (McLaren's 12-seat accelerator, Burch's live events)

## Step 2: Choose the right topic

- **Teach one narrow win.** A narrow topic works better than a full overview. SPI's member workshops each cover one skill. (Flynn, Burch)
- **Pick the problem your premium offer's buyers hit first.** The workshop removes the first obstacle. The offer handles the rest. (Mulready)
- **Break false beliefs in the teaching.** Structure the content as two or three "secrets." Each one knocks down a belief that would stop someone from buying the offer. (Brunson)
- **Teach the framework and leave out the full implementation.** Attendees learn what to do and see how much help would speed them up. Watch the line: if they leave with nothing usable, they won't trust you. (General workshop-to-application model, Fladlien's value-first rule)

- **Find the false beliefs by listing fears.** Write down 20 fears your audience has, turn each into a myth, and keep the best seven. Dispel them in the teaching without calling them myths. (Guzzi)

## Step 3: Build the machine

- **Charge for the workshop.** A small fee lifts show-up and builds a buyer list. (Stantons, Mulready)
- **Order bump ideas:** the replay, slides, a template pack, or a short implementation guide. (Hello Funnels, Laurie Burrows)
- **Thank-you page:** ask one question about who wants implementation help, and use the answers to sort your follow-up list. (Burch checklist)
- **Write the pitch as carefully as the lesson.** Script the transition, the stack, the price reveal, the guarantee, and the deadline. (Burch's pitch deck, Brunson)
- **Announce the offer at the start.** Say in the first five minutes that you'll share a way to work together at the end. (Fladlien)
- **Add a fast-action bonus** for people who buy during or right after the live session. (Stantons)
- **Email sequence:** confirmation, reminders (24 hours, 1 hour, 10 minutes, live now), replay, then a 4 to 5 day enrollment window with several emails on the final day. (Stantons, Brunson)
- **Use a sideways sales letter in pre-launch.** Send short pieces of content before the workshop that each answer one question and set up the next. (Walker)

- **Script the opening minutes.** Use a short recorded intro that ends with your name. Ask three questions the audience will say yes to, give a one-line table of contents, and say questions wait until the end. (Guzzi)
- **Build the funnel so it can be reused.** Tag each step (registered, attended, bought) to see where people drop off. Keep sales, onboarding, and follow-up sequences separate, and write reminder emails backward from the event date. The next run then needs only new dates. (Guzzi)

## Step 4: Drive traffic

- **Launch to your warm list first.** Refine the message there before buying traffic. (Walker's internal launch)
- **Run pre-launch on purpose.** Warm-up content in the weeks before cart open decides most of the result. (Porterfield)
- **Calculate the ad budget before you spend.** Revenue per registrant sets the ceiling on cost per registrant. (Burch's calculator, benchmarks.md)
- **Ask partners to co-host.** Borrow trust from someone whose audience matches yours. (Burch checklist)
- **Don't reuse warm-launch assets for cold traffic.** Cold audiences need more context and proof. (Porterfield)

- **Invite LinkedIn connections to the workshop.** Build a targeted list in Sales Navigator, connect with a friendly message, and offer a free seat instead of pitching in the chat. (Guzzi, Ashley)

## Step 5: Deliver, invite, and follow up

- **Give value first and sell second.** (Fladlien)
- **Pitch only what the workshop set up.** If the pitch drifts from the content, buyers stop trusting you. (Stantons)
- **Show what staying stuck costs them.** (Fladlien)
- **Hold a firm deadline and say so.** (Brunson)
- **Personally follow up with everyone who raised a hand.** Use the thank-you survey answers and chat activity. (Burch checklist)
- **Debrief against targets you set in advance.** Measure tickets, show-up rate, bump rate, and back-end sales. (Mulready, Burch)
- **Repeat on a schedule.** Running the same workshop on a regular cadence turns it into a dependable lead source. (Porterfield's guest with the weekly Monday webinar, McLaren's yearly series)
- **Hold questions until the end.** Hand out a sheet for questions and aha moments so people still feel heard. Open Q&A invites "will you run this again?", which stalls buying. (Guzzi)
- **Make the story's turning point match the offer.** Tell the mess, the turning point, and the results. When your turning point is the thing you sell, the pitch feels like the next scene. (Guzzi)
- **Read the room in person.** Teach toward the people responding most and let their energy spread. Aim for a few big laughs an hour. (Guzzi)
